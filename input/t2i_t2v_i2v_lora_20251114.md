# LoRA fine-tuning for T2I, T2V, and I2V models

## 1. Background and overview

- **T2V (text-to-video)** models such as Wan 2.2, HunyuanVideo, and related DiT-style video generators.
- **I2V (image-to-video)** models and T2V models in I2V mode (conditioning on a reference frame).

The emphasis is on **open-source models** and the **Hugging Face ecosystem**, combining:

- Official docs and blogs from Hugging Face and model authors.
- Model cards and Spaces for real-world LoRA checkpoints.
- GitHub repos and issues (Diffusers, kohya-ss, Musubi-tuner, AI Toolkit, etc.).
- Local project notes about FLUX and Wan 2.2 LoRA training, data preparation, and cost estimation.

### 1.1 What LoRA is and why it matters

**LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)** is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method. Instead of updating all model weights, you:

- Freeze the original model parameters.
- Add small low-rank matrices (LoRA adapters) to selected layers.
- Train only these adapters.

Key points:

- The **rank** `r` controls adapter capacity: higher rank → more parameters, more expressiveness, more VRAM.
- The scaling parameter **alpha** is often chosen so that **alpha ≈ rank**, making the effective scale `alpha / rank` roughly 1.
- Because you only train a tiny fraction of the model, LoRA can run even on a single consumer GPU and be stacked or merged at inference time.

The original LoRA paper and PEFT docs describe the general mechanism; Diffusers and video-specific trainers adapt this idea to image and video diffusion models.

### 1.2 T2I vs T2V vs I2V when fine-tuning

While the LoRA concept is shared, the **compute and data shape** differ:

- **T2I (image)**: each training sample is a single image plus caption. Resolution is typically 512×512 or 1024×1024 (or similar bucketing). This is the lightest case.
- **T2V (video)**: each sample is a clip of frames plus caption. Memory grows with resolution, number of frames, and the model size (e.g., Wan 2.2 14B). Training is more expensive.
- **I2V (image-to-video)**: usually the same architecture as T2V, but with an additional conditioning image. LoRA training for I2V typically uses video clips plus captions and sometimes explicit conditioning frames; VRAM is similar to T2V.

In practice, many **I2V LoRAs** are trained either:

- As **image-only LoRA** on a T2V base (teaching appearance via images; motion from the base model).
- Or as **full video LoRA** with short clips, where both motion and appearance are adapted.

## 5. Implementation patterns and tips

This section provides practical patterns for T2I, T2V, and I2V LoRA training, combining official docs, community defaults, and local notes.

### 5.1 Common concepts across T2I/T2V/I2V

Regardless of the model type:

1. **Data pairing**  
   - Images: `image.ext` + `image.txt` (caption) in the same folder.  
   - Videos: `clip_000.mp4` + `clip_000.txt` (caption).  
   - Most trainers expect same-name text files with `.txt` captions.

2. **Resolution and bucketing**  
   - T2I: set a base resolution (e.g., 512×512, 768×768, 1024×1024); use bucketing to handle varying aspect ratios.  
   - T2V/I2V: specify `resolution = [H, W]` (e.g., `[544, 960]` or `[720, 1280]`) and buckets based on frame counts (e.g., `target_frames = [17, 33, 65]`).

3. **Captioning**  
   - Use **clear, factual captions**; avoid noisy tags unless you know they help your model.  
   - For style or identity LoRAs, introduce a unique **trigger token** (`<my_style>` or a pseudo-name) in captions to control when the LoRA activates.

4. **Hyperparameters**  
   - **Rank**: 16–32 is a conservative starting point for T2I; 32–64 for more complex styles or video LoRAs.  
   - **Alpha**: often equal to rank (e.g., `alpha = 32` for `rank = 32`).  
   - **Learning rate**: `1e-4` for many T2I LoRAs; `2e-4` is common for Wan/Hunyuan video LoRAs.  
   - **Batch size**: small (1–2) with gradient accumulation to reach an effective batch of 4–8 or more.

5. **VRAM reduction techniques**  
   - Gradient checkpointing.  
   - 8-bit optimizers (e.g., 8-bit Adam).  
   - Caching latents and text-encoder outputs.  
   - QLoRA (4-bit base) for very large models (FLUX, big video models).  
   - FP8 training where available (for H100 or specialized setups).

6. **Early stopping and checkpoint selection**  
   - Save checkpoints frequently (every 100–500 steps or each epoch).  
   - Evaluate with a fixed validation prompt/clip set.  
   - Stop when identity/style looks good; additional steps often increase overfitting.

- Rank: 16–32; increase if underfitting.
- LR: 1e-4 or slightly lower (e.g., 5e-5) when training for many steps.

#### 5.3.3 Hyperparameters and VRAM for T2V

For Wan 2.2 T2V LoRA with Musubi-tuner-like trainers:

- Rank: `32` (alpha `16`) as a baseline; `64/32` for more detailed identities.
- Learning rate: `2e-4` for general likeness; up to `3e-4` if you only need motion/layout.
- Batch size: `1` on 16 GB; `1–2` on 24 GB with FP8 and block-swapping.
- VRAM:
  - Image LoRA: ~12 GB floor.
  - Video LoRA: ~24 GB recommended; with FP8 and block-swapping, 16 GB is possible at reduced speed.
- Advanced knobs:
  - `timestep_sampling` (`shift` or `sigmoid`) influences how noise levels are sampled and can affect likeness.
  - `discrete_flow_shift` around 2–3 for identity; adjust higher for more diversity.
  - Save each epoch; evaluate short clips regularly and stop when motion and appearance stabilize.

### 5.4 I2V LoRA patterns (Wan 2.2 I2V)

I2V LoRA for Wan 2.2 typically follows the T2V patterns with additional care for conditioning frames and artifacts:

1. **I2V vs T2V dataset**  
   - Many I2V training recipes still use video clips and captions like T2V.  
   - Some add explicit fields or modes to ensure the first frame is treated as a conditioning image.

2. **Common issues**  
   - Misconfigured “I2V mode” (wrong model variant or dataset flags) can cause errors where T2V training works but I2V fails.  
   - Very high epochs or excessive steps can introduce color and motion artifacts (e.g., unnatural “bloody” effects). Early stopping is critical.

3. **Hyperparameters**  
   - Often the same as T2V (rank 32–64, LR ~2e-4, batch size 1–2 with FP8 and block-swapping).  
   - I2V runs can be slightly more VRAM-demanding due to conditioning, so expect similar or slightly stricter VRAM needs than T2V.

4. **Inference patterns**  
   - In Wan2.2-Lightning models, inference commands show how to combine base checkpoints and LoRA directories for I2V tasks, using flags like `--task i2v-A14B`, `--size "1280*720"`, `--lora_dir`, and offload options.  
   - For single GPU inference, `--offload_model True`, `--convert_model_dtype`, and `--t5_cpu` can be used to fit large models with LoRA on consumer hardware.

Overall, treat I2V LoRAs as **video LoRAs** with stricter constraints on alignment between reference frames and motion, and validate with a range of I2V prompts to ensure robustness.

## 6. Limitations, caveats, and open questions

1. **Model-family compatibility**  
   - LoRAs are usually **not portable across base families** (e.g., SD 1.5 LoRA cannot be used on SDXL or FLUX; Wan T2V LoRA is not compatible with unrelated models). Use LoRAs with the same base family and architecture they were trained on.

2. **Resolution mismatches**  
   - Training at 512 or 768 and inferring at 1024 is common for SD and FLUX. This usually works but may change the “strength” of the LoRA, especially for fine detail. If you need precise 1024 behavior, consider at least part of training at or near 1024 (with reduced batch size).

3. **Video-specific artifacts**  
   - For Wan 2.2 I2V, community issues report strange artifacts (like “bloody” frames) at high epochs or when noise settings are misconfigured. Careful monitoring and step-by-step validation are required.
   - Inconsistent fps/resolution across clips can degrade motion or break training; keep them uniform.

4. **Speed and cost**  
   - SD 1.5 LoRA is cheapest and most forgiving (~5h for 15k steps on 11 GB GPU in the reference example).  
   - SDXL LoRA at 1024 is significantly heavier and may require 24 GB VRAM for comfortable workflows.  
   - FLUX.1-dev LoRA without quantization is extremely heavy; QLoRA reduces this to single-GPU viability but still involves substantial compute.  
   - Wan 2.2 video LoRAs require strong GPUs (24 GB+) or aggressive VRAM optimizations (FP8, block-swapping, offload) and longer wall-clock times.

5. **Tool and ecosystem churn**  
   - Musubi-tuner, AI Toolkit, DiffSynth-Studio, and FLUX trainers are actively evolving. Command-line flags, default configs, and performance characteristics may shift over time, so always check recent docs and issues.

6. **Evaluation and generalization**  
   - LoRAs trained on narrow datasets may overfit to specific prompts, poses, or motions.  
   - Best practice is to hold out some prompts and clips, evaluating periodically during training, and to favor the best mid-run checkpoint rather than the final step.

### 7.2 SDXL T2I LoRA (1024px)

- Base: `stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0` with fixed SDXL VAE.
- Data: 200–2,000 images at 1024px or bucketed around 1024.
- Hyperparameters:
  - Rank: 16–32, alpha = rank.
  - LR: 1e-4 or slightly lower.
  - Steps: 1k–3k.
- Environment:
  - 24 GB GPU strongly recommended at 1024.
  - Or ~11–12 GB with gradient checkpointing, cached text encodings, and small batch sizes.

### 7.3 FLUX.1-dev T2I LoRA (QLoRA)

- Base: `black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-dev` in 4-bit QLoRA configuration.
- Data: curated 500–1,000 images at 512×768, balanced across backgrounds and lighting.
- Hyperparameters:
  - Rank: ~32, alpha ~32.
  - LR: 1e-4 constant, short warmup.
  - Steps: ~700–2,000.
- Environment:
  - 9–10 GB GPU with QLoRA, gradient checkpointing, cached latents, and 8-bit Adam.

### 7.4 Wan 2.2 T2V LoRA

- Base: Wan 2.2 T2V model (e.g., A14B variant).
- Data:
  - Clips around 544×960, 24 fps, ~2 seconds long (17–65 frames).
  - Same-name `.mp4` + `.txt` caption pairs; captions ≥50 words.
- Hyperparameters (Musubi-tuner style):
  - Rank: 32, alpha 16 (starting point); 64/32 for more complex identity.
  - LR: 2e-4 baseline.
  - Batch size: 1 (16 GB) or 1–2 (24 GB) with FP8 and block-swapping.
- Environment:
  - Ideally 24 GB+ GPU for smooth training.
  - FP8 and block-swapping to run on 16 GB, with slower steps.

### 7.5 Wan 2.2 I2V LoRA

- Base: Wan 2.2 I2V model (e.g., I2V 14B variant).
- Data: same as T2V, but configured in I2V mode within Musubi-tuner/AI Toolkit, ensuring correct condition handling for the first frame.
- Hyperparameters:
  - Same as T2V LoRA (rank 32–64, LR ~2e-4, batch 1–2).
  - Emphasis on early stopping to avoid high-epoch artifacts.
- Environment:
  - Similar VRAM needs as T2V; pay attention to additional memory from conditioning.
