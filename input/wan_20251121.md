### 4.4 Color flicker and VAE issues in ComfyUI (Wan 2.x)

Your `wan_color_issue.md` document aggregates multiple bug reports and fixes from GitHub issues, Reddit threads, and ComfyUI docs. The key findings:

1. **Tiled VAE Decode** is a common cause of color flicker / blinking with Wan 2.2 output:
   - Using `VAE Decode (Tiled)` in ComfyUI can introduce frame-to-frame color shifts, especially at the **start and end** of clips.
   - Switching to the non-tiled `VAE Decode` node often eliminates the issue, at the cost of higher VRAM usage.
   - Github issues (e.g., `comfyanonymous/ComfyUI#7116`) and Reddit posts about Wan 2.1/2.2 confirm this behavior.

2. **Mis-matched VAE** can cause discoloration and artifacts:
   - It is important to decode with the **official Wan 2.2 VAE** specified in the ComfyUI native guide, not an SDXL or other VAE.
   - Loading Wan 2.1 VAE with Wan 2.2 models, or vice versa, can produce tensor-shape errors or subtle color shifts.

3. **First/last-second glitches**:
   - Several users observe a brief flash or color glitch in the **first or last second** of videos from Wan 2.x, even with correct settings.
   - Workarounds include trimming the first/last second or re-decoding without tiling.

4. **ColorMatch darkening (MVGD)**:
   - Some ColorMatch methods (e.g., **MVGD** in KJNodes “Easy Use” ColorMatch) tend to **darken** the overall luminance of the video.
   - Alternative methods like **Reinhard**, **MKL**, or compound passes (e.g., **HM–MVGD–HM**, **HM–MKL–HM**) are less likely to globally darken the clip.

Your distilled “A/B recipe” for Wan 2.2 color issues:

1. Keep your existing Kijai or native Wan workflow.
2. Replace `VAE Decode (Tiled)` with standard `VAE Decode` (or use a lower resolution / quantization so non-tiled fits in VRAM).
3. Ensure the VAE is the **official Wan 2.2 VAE** from the ComfyUI docs for that model.
4. If using ColorMatch, change method from **MVGD → Reinhard**, and, if necessary, test MKL or compound combinations.

If glitches persist, the next debugging steps are to share exact decode nodes, VAE file paths, resolution, and whether VRAM pressure forces tiling or hybrid offload.

## 6. LoRA fine-tuning recipes for Wan 2.2 (T2V and I2V)

This section summarizes practical LoRA recipes for Wan 2.2 T2V and I2V, combining official docs, Musubi-tuner guidance, AI Toolkit configs, and your notes.

### 6.1 T2V LoRA (text-to-video)

**Goal:** Teach Wan 2.2 T2V a new style or character using short video clips or images driven by text prompts.

**Minimal but realistic setup (Musubi-tuner, 14B T2V):**

- **Data**:
  - Option A: **Video LoRA** – multiple short clips (2–4 seconds) at ~544×960, 24 fps, per-video captions of 50+ words.
  - Option B: **Image-only LoRA** – curated images, each with a descriptive caption; Wan base model’s motion is reused.

- **Hyperparameters**:
  - Rank / alpha: `r = 32`, `alpha = 16` (increase to 64/32 if underfitting).
  - LR: `2e-4` with warmup, cosine or linear decay.
  - Batch size: `1` (16 GB) or `1–2` (24 GB+).
  - Training steps: often in the few-thousand range; monitor validation for overfitting (e.g., loss flattening + overly stylized outputs).

- **VRAM tricks**:
  - Enable **FP8** for backbone, **block-swapping**, and **caching** of latents and text-encoder outputs.
  - Use lower temporal resolution (fewer frames) and spatial resolution if VRAM is tight.

**Inference notes**:

- Apply LoRAs at moderate weights; stack them with Lightning LoRAs or control adapters if needed, but expect compounding effects.
- For character LoRAs, keep prompts descriptive but not overloaded with style tokens; many community examples highlight the importance of **balanced prompt engineering**.

### 6.2 I2V LoRA (image-to-video)

**Goal:** Teach Wan 2.2 I2V to animate a particular style or character given a reference frame.

From your LoRA KB and Wan-specific notes:

- **Base model**: Wan 2.2 I2V variant (e.g., `Wan2.2-I2V-A14B`).
- **Data**:
  - Similar to T2V, but configured as I2V in Musubi-tuner or AI Toolkit so that the first frame acts as conditioning.
  - Many practitioners still use **image-only LoRA** on the I2V backbone: images teach appearance; motion is inherited from the pretrained I2V model.
- **Hyperparameters**:
  - Same as T2V LoRA: `rank = 32–64`, `alpha = 16–32`, `lr ≈ 2e-4`, batch size 1–2.
  - Early stopping is especially important: high epoch counts can cause flicker and unstable motion after a few seconds.
- **VRAM**:
  - Similar to T2V LoRA; conditioning adds a small amount of extra memory but does not dominate compared to the backbone.

**Practical I2V tips**:

- In Musubi-tuner configs, ensure that I2V mode is correctly enabled and that the first frame is treated as conditioning, not just part of a video clip.
- For consistent character LoRAs, community advice is to use **mixed datasets**:
  - Still images for clean identity (face/character) supervision.
  - Short clips for motion and pose diversity.
  - Control weighting via `num_repeats` in `dataset.toml`.

## 7. Limitations, caveats, and open questions

1. **Model and API stability**:
   - Wan 2.2’s open-source ecosystem is evolving quickly; scripts and model layouts may change between releases.
   - Diffusers integration is not as standardized as for SDXL or FLUX; most Wan work still relies on custom trainers like Musubi-tuner, AI Toolkit, or DiffSynth-Studio.

2. **Hardware demands**:
   - Even with 5B TI2V variants, Wan 2.2 is at the upper end of what 8–12 GB GPUs can handle, especially for longer clips or multiple users.
   - FP8 and GGUF variants, plus caching and offloading, make things feasible but introduce more moving pieces and potential failure modes.

3. **Color and stability issues**:
   - Tiled VAE decode and mis-matched VAEs are known sources of flicker and color shifts with Wan.
   - Some color artifacts may be inherent to current Wan training/inference pipelines; trimming and conservative ColorMatch settings are pragmatic workarounds.

4. **LoRA complexity**:
   - Wan 2.2 LoRAs can be more sensitive to rank, LR, and dataset composition than SD/SDXL LoRAs due to the larger backbone and MoE structure.
   - There is still active experimentation on best practices for motion vs appearance LoRAs, especially for longer cinematic clips.

5. **Licensing and usage constraints**:
   - Always check the **license and usage sections** of Wan 2.2 model cards; some variants may have commercial-use restrictions or require attribution.

6. **Evaluation and benchmarks**:
   - Unlike SDXL or FLUX, Wan 2.2 does not yet have a widely standardized benchmark suite for LoRA quality and motion consistency.
   - Community practice relies heavily on qualitative inspection of sample videos, which can make apples-to-apples comparisons difficult.
